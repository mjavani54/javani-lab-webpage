Research / Four connected directions

Physics first. Computation with purpose.

Our work connects nanophotonic design, interpretable AI, quantum algorithms, and security. Each direction asks what information matters, what resources it takes to use it, and how to verify the result.

01 / Light & matter

AI for nanophotonics

We investigate how machine learning can accelerate the design of optical structures without hiding the physics. Studies include multilayer thin films, metasurfaces, and gratings, with attention to inverse design, model pruning, feature importance, and low-dimensional representations.

The central question is not only whether a model predicts a spectrum. It is whether the model exposes the structure of the design problem and earns its computational cost.

02 / Quantum systems

Quantum computing for physical models

We explore resource-efficient quantum algorithms for molecular and other physical systems. Current computational studies compare variational methods with exact references, examine compact circuit designs, and test how results behave when the physical model changes.

Claims about advantage or accuracy require an honest accounting of circuit resources, classical baselines, and the assumptions behind each comparison.

Variational algorithmsQuantum chemistryCircuit resourcesValidation
03 / Scientific intelligence

AI for scientific discovery

Scientific models should help reveal principles, not just fit data. We study interpretable learning, dimensionality reduction, and computational searches for patterns that transfer between related physical designs and quantum problems.

Our current questions include how to identify the smallest useful representation of a system and how to distinguish a genuine transferable insight from a convenient fit to one dataset.

Representation learningDimensionalityExplainable AITransfer
04 / Trust & resilience

Quantum and cyber security

We are developing research directions in quantum cryptography and resilient cyber-physical systems. The focus is on testable security claims: clear threat models, independently checked benchmarks, and adaptive defense strategies that account for learning adversaries.

This work connects computation with the realities of deploying and evaluating protection in complex systems.

Quantum cryptographyCyber-physical systemsAdaptive defenseAudits

Rigor is part of the design.

Our research process favors explicit assumptions, reproducible computation, and comparisons that make limitations visible. Strong results begin with a precise question and end with evidence others can examine.
