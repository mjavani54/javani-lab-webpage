About the lab

Built for questions that cross fields.

Javani Research Lab brings together methods from physics, electrical engineering, computer science, and security to investigate complex systems with clarity and rigor.

Principal investigator

Mohammad H. Javani, Ph.D.

Dr. Javani is an Assistant Professor in the Department of Cyber-Physical Systems at Clark Atlanta University. His research spans computational nanophotonics, machine learning for physical design, quantum computing, and cybersecurity.

His training across physics and electrical and computer engineering shapes a research style that moves between physical models and computational methods. The lab studies how to make algorithms more efficient, interpretable, and reliable—and how to determine when a result has truly earned its claim.

He mentors students working across disciplinary boundaries and welcomes collaborations that connect fundamental questions with rigorous computation.

Working principles

Questions before claims.

01 / PHYSICS

Ground the model

Anchor computational methods in the system they are meant to explain.

02 / INTERPRETATION

Understand the result

Ask what a model learned and which details genuinely matter.

03 / EVIDENCE

Test the limits

Use clear baselines, resource accounting, and independent checks.

Students & collaborators

Work on questions worth checking.

We also welcome collaboration inquiries from researchers working at the boundaries of these areas.
